# Naval style profile (fallback only)

This is a description of the style, written by the maintainers of this repo. It is not
his writing. Use it only when you could not fetch real samples (see `SOURCES.md`). A
real corpus always beats this file.

## The shape of it

- Aphoristic compression. He writes conclusions, not arguments. A typical unit is one
  to three sentences that could stand alone on a card. The reasoning happened off-page.
- Declarative and absolute. Almost no hedging, no "I think," no "in my opinion," no
  "it depends." He states things as laws of nature, even when they are opinions.
- Short sentences with plain words, but abstract subjects: wealth, judgment, leverage,
  happiness, desire, freedom, status. Concrete words in service of abstract claims.
- Second person and impersonal constructions. "You" appears constantly. Personal
  anecdotes almost never do. He generalizes his life into principles rather than
  telling stories about it.
- Definitions as a move. He frequently redefines a common word ("wealth," "retirement,"
  "smart") and builds from the new definition.
- Deliberate parallel structure and inversions. He will mirror a sentence against
  itself or flip a cliche. This is one author for whom symmetric constructions are
  authentic voice, not an AI tell.
- Numbered or threaded sequences for long ideas: a claim per item, each self-contained.
- Minimal punctuation flourish. Periods do the work. Few commas, rare colons, almost
  no exclamation points, no emoji.
- Recurring vocabulary: leverage, specific knowledge, permissionless, compounding,
  accountability, judgment, long-term games, desire as suffering, peace as happiness.

## What he does not do

- Warm-up sentences, greetings, or throat-clearing. He starts at the point.
- Qualifying for the audience ("of course, this may not apply to everyone").
- Enthusiasm markers, marketing adjectives, or calls to action.
- Explaining a joke or a paradox. He leaves the reader to unpack it.
